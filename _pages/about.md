Hi there! I'm Rose.

I'm a third-year PhD student in Experimental Psychology at UC San Diego, working in the Developmental Area with Dr. Gail Heyman and Dr. Caren Walker.

My research sits at the intersection of language development, pragmatics, and social cognition. I'm interested in how children can learn so much from so little, and how language shapes what they think about people and the world around them.

My current work focuses on pragmatic inference as a mechanism for social learning. I look at how linguistic and communicative cues — things like the explanations adults offer, the words we choose to describe people, or the way praise is framed — shape children's inferences about normativity, ability, and social group membership.

I'm a proud Appalachia native and completed my undergraduate education at Chatham University in Pittsburgh, PA. Before coming to UCSD, I spent four years as the lab manager for Dr. Alison Gopnik's Cognitive Development lab at UC Berkeley, where I fell even more deeply in love with the weird and wonderful things kids know!

Beyond my research, I work on evaluating and improving the safety of AI systems. I also write about complex technical and scientific topics, from public-facing copy to materials for policymakers, lawyers, and government.

Outside of the lab, you can find me on the beach with a good book or on the couch with a bad movie. I live in sunny San Diego with my two cats, Purrogi and Squeaker, who are <s>awful</s> excellent research assistants.
